All The Way Through

I visited Lindey's Lake House on Saturday after seeing The Lion King, and unfortunately, my experience was extremely disappointing. Our party of eight was seated, and we were advised upfront that we would have one check with an automatic gratuity included. Half of our party was seated initially, and the rest arrived about 15 minutes later. Our server was pleasant and seemed friendly, but there were several issues throughout the evening that made the experience frustrating.

From the beginning, there were problems with orders being received correctly and in a timely manner. I ordered a jalapeño margarita, but when it finally arrived, it came out as a pitcher rather than a single drink. I had to wait a significant amount of time for the correct drink replacement while dinner continued. Food also came out with large gaps between guests receiving their meals, which was frustrating, although everyone at the table remained patient, understanding, and respectful. There were also smaller things that added up, such as ordering biscuits and never receiving them until I had to ask about them toward the very end of the meal.

The biggest frustration came when trying to close out the bill. Since it was one check, we needed some time to sort out the payment between the group, and I completely understood that our server was giving us that time. However, even after we had figured everything out, I waited a significant amount of time to be cashed out. I asked another staff member if they could assist since my server was unavailable, but I was told they could not, so I sat back down and continued waiting. While I was sitting there waiting, another host approached the table to seat new guests and asked me to get up, which was extremely frustrating considering I was still waiting to finish paying. The entire situation made me feel overlooked and unsure of what was happening. When the payment was eventually processed, there was also confusion that resulted in my card being charged again, and I now have to wait for the money to be returned. This could have been avoided with clearer communication during the payment process.

A manager/supervisor did come over earlier when we were experiencing issues with the food, and the assistant manager who helped later with the payment issue was kind and seemed to genuinely care. However, overall, the experience felt disorganized and as though there wasn't much urgency or empathy shown toward our table.

I have worked in restaurants, so I understand that busy nights happen and mistakes occur. What makes a difference is how those situations are handled. When guests are patient and understanding despite inconveniences, it goes a long way to have someone acknowledge the issues, show empathy, and make them feel heard rather than making them feel like a burden. Unfortunately, after this experience, I don't feel inclined to return.

I don't believe our server was intentionally providing poor service, and I recognize she may have had a lot going on, but the overall lack of communication, follow-through, and guest care made what should have been a nice evening out a very frustrating one.

What Good Looks Like
✓
Big tables get a manager, early
Parties of six or more get a manager touch at drink drop and entrée drop — not after it breaks.
✓
Read the drink order back
Single or pitcher, rocks or up, spicy or not. Ten seconds at the table beats fifteen minutes of recovery.
✓
The table eats together
If a plate is going to lag, tell the table before the first one lands. No surprises.
✓
Anyone can run a check
If a guest is holding a card, take it. “Not my section” is not an answer here.
✓
Never move a seated guest
Hosts clear every turn with the server or a manager before approaching the table.
✓
Own it out loud
Acknowledge the miss, apologize once, fix it, and come back. Silence reads as indifference.
Hospitality: the friendly and generous reception and entertainment of guests.
