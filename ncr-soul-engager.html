Managers Working the Floor
More than one review this week called out a manager by name for stepping onto the floor, resetting a seating plan, or delivering the dessert personally. That's leadership showing up as service, not supervision from a distance.
2
Servers Who Made the Meal Feel Personal
Guests kept naming names this week. Pacing a table perfectly, remembering a returning guest, checking in more than once, these are small choices that turn a transaction into a memory guests want to repeat.

Signature Dishes Landing Clean
The branzino, the pollo al limón, the lemon parmesan chicken, the wedge salad, all praised by name, all praised specifically. When a guest can describe exactly why a dish worked, the kitchen executed it right.
NCR Ventures 3 Opportunities to Be Better Week Ending July 5, 2026 — When Guests Remember Your Name
5 of 5
1
The First Face a Guest Sees
More than one review this week opened with a host who didn't smile, didn't make eye contact, or delivered a policy without warmth. That first ten seconds sets the tone for everything after it, and it's the cheapest fix on this list.
2
The Check-In That Disappears After the Ticket Prints
A strong opening, then silence until the check arrives. That pattern showed up more than once this week, across different concepts. A guest shouldn't have to flag someone down for a refill they already asked for.
3
Comfort Issues Guests Notice Before We Do
A few locations had air conditioning or facility issues that guests flagged in their reviews before the fix caught up. Faster internal reporting on comfort means guests stop being the ones who tell us first.
