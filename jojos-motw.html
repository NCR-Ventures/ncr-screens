Pasta, Made Here
Made In-House · Cut Daily · Chef Silvia's New Recipe
The Story

Chef Silvia flew in from Italy last week and left us a new dough, and it went on the line the same week. Every noodle you see was mixed, rested, and cut in our kitchen — extruded for the short shapes, coiled into nests for the long ones.

Fresh is not automatically better than dry. It is different. Fresh is soft and silky and carries butter, cream, and ragù. Dry semolina is firm and rough and stands up to oil, tomato, and hard tossing in the pan. Know which one is in your hand and cook it like it deserves.

Dry · The Box
  • Semolina and water — Italian law, 1967
  • Eight to twelve minutes to al dente
  • Firm and rough. Built for oil, tomato, and hard tossing
By the Numbers · Italy
The average Italian puts away about 50 pounds of pasta a year, more than any country on earth. Almost all of it comes out of a box. Fresh is the one they make when it matters.
