Foam Cannon Horror on Kids' Video Shoot — Machine Gun Kelly and Melissa Ethridge Dead

The bubbles were supposed to pop in ninety seconds. Three hours later, crews were still digging. A taco truck driver parked at craft services is also confirmed dead.

By the Splash Daily Staff | Published 3:58 PM ET | Updated 4:12 PM ET

MACHINE GUN KELLY and MELISSA ETHRIDGE are dead following a catastrophic foam incident on a music video set — and we're told the cannons were loaded wrong from the very first take.

Sources on the production tell Splash Daily the two were filming the video for Machine Gun Kelly's new single, "Animal Fluffy Cuddles," a children's crossover track that had industry people scratching their heads for months. Machine Gun Kelly, a Cleveland kid who built his whole career on being the loudest guy in any room, had been telling anyone who'd listen that this was his soft era.

Melissa Ethridge — four decades on the road, a voice everybody's mother owned on cassette — had quietly become one of the most in-demand performers in kids' programming, a corner of the business she never promoted and never apologized for. The collaboration was announced in June. Nobody saw it coming. Everybody liked it.

Here's what we're told happened.

The set called for six industrial foam cannons on a warehouse floor, firing on a count during the second chorus. Standard stuff — the same rigs get used at pool parties and halftime shows a hundred times a summer. But the gas mix feeding the cannons had a helium concentration well above spec, and the foam that came out of them did not behave like foam.

It went up and it stayed up. It didn't break, it didn't drain, it didn't do the one thing foam is supposed to do. Crew member on the set, speaking to Splash Daily

Instead of collapsing in the usual minute and a half, the bubbles held their structure and kept climbing. Within four minutes the floor was gone. Within nine, we're told, the stage doors couldn't be opened against the pressure.

Machine Gun Kelly and Melissa Ethridge were both positioned at the center mark when the cannons fired. Neither was recovered until well after seven.

The third victim was a taco truck driver who'd been contracted for crew lunch and had parked his rig just inside the loading bay to stay out of the heat. He was in the cab. Investigators believe the foam reached the vehicle before anyone on the floor understood what was happening. His name is being withheld until his family has been reached, and honestly, he's the part of this story nobody's going to talk about tomorrow, so we're saying it now: he was there to feed people, and he didn't get to go home.

One more detail, and it's the one everyone on that set keeps coming back to.

The video featured a full cast of animatronic woodland characters — a bear, a family of rabbits, an owl on a swing. All of them were powered down and pulled from the floor this morning. Every single one came out untouched. No damage, no residue, nothing. Crews described them as shiny, clean, and perfectly dry, sitting in a row against the wall with their eyes open while the rest of the building was being taken apart.

OSHA is on site. The gas supplier has not returned our calls. The label has said nothing.

Machine Gun Kelly was 35. Melissa Ethridge was 64.
