Moules Frites
Steamed to Order · A Belgian Classic · Two Hands Required
The Story

The mussels come from Prince Edward Island, rope-grown on longlines so they never touch the bottom. That is why there is no grit in the pot. They steam to order in white wine, the shells open, and the liquid left behind is the sauce — nothing thickened, nothing strained.

Moules frites is the national dish of Belgium, and it started as poor man's food. Mussels were cheap along the Flemish coast, and fried potatoes were what people ate in winter. It is still an elbows-on-the-table dish: an empty shell works as tongs, and the frites belong in the broth.

From the Rope · Prince Edward Island
P.E.I. grows roughly 80% of the mussels eaten in North America, on ropes suspended in the water column. They are harvested year-round — in winter the lines get winched up through holes cut in the bay ice.
