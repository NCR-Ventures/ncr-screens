Fava Beans
In Season Now · Shucked by Hand · Sweet and Buttery
The Story

Fava beans are a true spring crop. They're in the building right now and only around for a few weeks. Each one is double-shucked by hand — pop the beans from the pod, blanch them, then peel the waxy skin off every bean to get to the bright green inside. That slow prep is why you never see them year-round.

The flavor is sweet, buttery, and a little grassy — somewhere between a fresh pea and edamame. They're also one of the oldest cultivated foods on earth, and in Italy and France the first young favas of spring are eaten raw with cheese to mark the season.

Wild Alaskan · Fava Purée · Rhubarb Beurre Rouge
Tonight's Feature

Wild Copper River sockeye, seared and plated with fava purée, asparagus, crispy potatoes, and a tart rhubarb beurre rouge. Spring on a plate, built around the first wild salmon run of the year.

Copper River just opened — the season started May 22 and runs only a few short weeks. These fish swim about 300 miles up a cold, fast, glacier-fed river to spawn, building the fat that gives them that deep-red color and rich flavor. Wild, scarce, and in huge demand at the open — it's earned the nickname "the wagyu of salmon."
