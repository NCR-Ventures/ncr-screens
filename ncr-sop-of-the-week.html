The Pre-Shift
The single highest-leverage fifteen minutes a manager gets all day.
Governing Principle
Two pre-shifts a day, minimum. Ten to fifteen minutes. Standing up. Phones away. Teach one thing, taste one thing, and close on the one thing. It is not an announcement. It is a rehearsal. Don't skip it. Don't wing it. Lead it.

The Run of Show — Fifteen Minutes, Six Blocks
Same six blocks, same order, every building, every service. Content changes daily. Structure never does.
0:00–1:00
Line-Up
Stand in a line, full uniform, phones away. Walk the line: appearance, pen, wine key, crumber, lighter, and attitude. Eye contact with every person.
1:00–4:00
The Board
Sections, covers, pacing, large parties, events. VIPs and regulars by name. Allergies on the book. 86s, low counts, push items. Features with the price.
4:00–8:00
Teach One Thing
Chef presents the feature: ingredients, how it is cooked, why it is great, what to drink with it. Taste it. Two people repeat it back as if to a guest.
8:00–11:00
The Hospitality Moment
One real guest review read aloud, good or bad. The focus for the week. A Soul Engager shout-out. The challenge: create one magic moment tonight.
11:00–13:00
Numbers and the Game
Yesterday's sales against the same day last year. Where the building is for the week. The contest for the shift, with a prize worth winning.
13:00–15:00
Open the Floor and Close
Sixty seconds for questions. Then ask three people: "What is the one thing tonight?" Same answer from all three means it worked. Break to stations.
THE ONE THING.
Every pre-shift ends with one thing. Not four. One. Sell the branzino. Greet every table in thirty seconds. If a server can't say it in one sentence at the close, the manager runs the close again. Never ends on a negative.

How it flows: Monday Focus Call → GM Call and Chef Call → Store L-10 → Pre-Shift → The Guest.
