Heavenly Biscuits
Baked From Scratch · Honey Butter · Limited Daily Batch
The Story

Biscuits are chemistry, not cooking. Cold butter cut into the flour leaves solid pieces in the dough; those pieces hit the oven, the water in them turns to steam, and the steam pushes the layers apart. Warm butter blends in instead of staying separate, and you get a scone. Keep the butter cold and handle the dough as little as you can.

They bake in the skillet and score into wedges, so the pull-apart edges stay soft while the top and the pan side go golden. Honey butter goes on hot, off the oven — it should be melting into the seams by the time the skillet lands on the table.

Why They Rise · The Steam
A biscuit gets almost all of its lift from water turning to steam, not from the leavening. That is also why they do not hold: once the steam cooks out and the crumb cools, no amount of reheating brings the texture back. Bake to the pace of the room.
